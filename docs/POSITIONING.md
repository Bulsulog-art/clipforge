# DentVideo: positioning and pricing

Written 2026-11-08 to answer the owner's question: **"Why would a dentist pay us $50/month?"**

**Short answer:** because the alternatives cost more, in money or in time. A dental social-media agency usually costs
**hundreds to thousands per month**: about $800–3,000 in the US, €300–1,500 in Spain, from €349 in Germany, and
12,000–65,000 TL in Turkey for clinic packages. Most agencies also want a minimum term. Doing it yourself with Canva, CapCut or an AI-avatar tool costs
**$15–30/month plus 5–10 hours a week**. Neither checks every line against health-advertising rules before it reaches the dentist.
DentVideo delivers five finished, checked videos every Monday for a few minutes of the dentist's time. At $50/month
the price is easy to defend. **We are more likely underpriced at $50 than overpriced.** I recommend using ~$49/€49 as the *founding*
price and listing the core plan at €79 / $89 / £69 (details below).

All figures below come from public web pages (mostly vendor and agency pages, which have a commercial interest). Treat them as
list-price benchmarks, not audited market data. Sources are listed at the end.

## 1. Ideal customer (ICP)

**Primary:** an owner-dentist of an independent private practice with 1–3 chairs and at least one elective, high-value
service: implants, clear aligners, whitening/veneers or paediatric. They have an Instagram account that is dormant or posts
irregularly. They know they "should" be posting. They have no in-house marketer, or the receptionist or office manager posts when they have time. Surveys show
office managers run social media in 28% of practices and dentists themselves in 23%
([1-800-DENTIST via DrBicuspid](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-business/dental-education/article/15365828/1-800-dentist-report-focuses-on-dental-office-managers)).

- **Markets, in order of readiness:** Turkey, Germany, Spain, UK, France, US.
  - **Turkey:** large private sector, Instagram-heavy, and a new 2025 promotion regulation that makes people afraid of breaking it.
  - **Germany:** high case values, and the fear of an *Abmahnung* (a formal cease-and-desist letter) under the HWG is real.
  - **Spain:** many private clinics, and agencies are relatively cheap, so the price has to stay low.
  - **UK and France:** strict professional codes.
  - **US:** biggest wallets, but many competitors at $99–299 for done-for-you posts.
- **Secondary:** small groups (2–5 sites) that want one consistent, compliant voice across locations and languages. For
  example, Turkish clinics serving German- or English-speaking patients.
- **Not a fit (for now):** DSOs with in-house marketing teams, and practices that want paid-ads management. We do not run ads.

## 2. Top three pains

1. **No time, so no consistency.** Scripting, filming, editing and captioning a weekly video takes hours. A Dentaltown guide puts
   DIY social at 5–10 hours a week ([Dentaltown](https://www.dentaltown.com/blog/post/4881/how-much-time-should-a-dentist-spend-on-social-media-marketing-their-practice-podcast-listen)).
   Older surveys found most practices spend under an hour a week, so the account goes quiet
   ([DentistryIQ](https://dentistryiq.com/practice-management/marketing/article/16354158/you-may-think-you-know-but-what-are-dental-practices-really-doing-with-social-media-we-have-the-answers)).
2. **Fear of breaking advertising rules.** One "painless", "guaranteed", a price or a before-and-after can lead to a complaint or a fine:
   - **Germany:** the Heilmittelwerbegesetz (§ 11 HWG) restricts before-and-after imagery. The BGH (I ZR 170/24, 31 Jul 2025) and
     OLG Frankfurt (6 U 40/25, 2025) ruled against Instagram before-and-after content for aesthetic procedures. Competitors
     and the Wettbewerbszentrale send Abmahnungen.
     ([Ärzteblatt 2026](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/archiv/titel/dae/2026/4/aerztliches-werberecht-vorsicht-auf-social-media-6eb5710d-c3c4-4c72-8951-e55e60f54bbb),
     [Rödl & Partner](https://www.roedl.com/insights/olg-frankfurt-a-m-story-sequenzen-koennen-verbotene-vorher-nachher-werbung-sein/),
     [iww.de](https://www.iww.de/zp/recht/heilmittelwerbegesetz-vergleichende-werbung-mit-vorher-nachher-bildern-bleibt-verboten-f170555)).
     The professional codes (Berufsordnung) of the regional dental chambers apply on top of the HWG.
   - **Turkey:** *Sağlık Hizmetlerinde Tanıtım ve Bilgilendirme Faaliyetleri Hakkında Yönetmelik*, Resmî Gazete
     12 Nov 2025, replacing the 2023 text. It bans advertising by physicians and dentists, including on social media, and permits
     only informative content. Before-and-after images, prices and campaigns are out. Breaches carry administrative fines, and repeat
     breaches at a facility can lead to suspension
     ([Kadim Hukuk](https://kadimhukuk.com.tr/makale/saglikta-reklam-yasagi-cezalari/),
     [Hukuki Haber](https://www.hukukihaber.net/hekimler-sosyal-medyada-neleri-paylasabilir),
     [Lexpera text](https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/yonetmelikler/saglik-hizmetlerinde-tanitim-ve-bilgilendirme-faaliyetleri-hakkinda-yonetmelik-1)).
     Check article numbers against the Resmî Gazete before quoting them.
   - **UK:** GDC guidance on advertising and on social media, plus the CAP Code, which the ASA enforces. Botulinum toxin
     (a prescription-only medicine) cannot be advertised to the public. A 2021 BDJ audit found **no** compliant Instagram accounts in
     its sample ([BDJ 2021](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/s41415-021-2718-4),
     [DDU](https://theddu.com/guidance-and-advice/guides/advertising-your-practice-ethically)).
   - **France:** Décret 2020-1658 / art. R.4127-215-1 CSP allows *information* but not *publicité*. The Ordre repeated in
     Dec 2025 that the decree "did not open the door to advertising". Comparisons with other dentists and third-party testimonials are excluded
     ([ONCD recommandations](https://www.information-dentaire.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/publicite-recommandations-ordinales-06052021.pdf),
     [ONCD Lettre 226](https://www.ordre-chirurgiens-dentistes.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ONCD-LA-LETTRE-226.pdf)).
   - **Spain:** Real Decreto 1907/1996 on health advertising, plus regional (autonomous community) rules and the Consejo
     General de Dentistas' code. The Tribunal Supremo upheld a ban on using celebrities in dental-clinic advertising
     ([Poder Judicial](https://www.poderjudicial.es/cgpj/es/Poder-Judicial/Noticias-Judiciales/El-Tribunal-Supremo-avala-la-prohibicion-de-difundir-publicidad-de-clinicas-de-odontologia-utilizando-a-personas-famosas),
     [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/epub/1996/BOE-A-1996-18085-consolidado.epub)).
3. **Paying an agency without seeing a return.** Retainers are high and come with minimum terms. Agencies also need briefings and feedback rounds, and
   the dentist still has to sign off. Few agencies publish prices: one review found only 4 of 19 did
   ([Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/best-social-media-management-agencies-for-dental)).

**What they care about:** new-patient bookings for elective treatment, case acceptance (patients who arrive already informed),
and trust ("they feel they know me"). Evidence that social media drives the choice of dentist is real but
regional and small-sample:
- A 2025 BMC Oral Health study (552 adults, Saudi Arabia) found Instagram a significant platform for choosing a dentist.
- An Indian survey found about half say a dentist's account activity affects their choice.
- For implants, 23.9% first heard about the treatment on social media.

([BMC Oral Health 2025](https://doaj.org/article/a2c57183aebd4cfcbb162732595186b0),
[JIAPHD 2022](https://doaj.org/article/142ae1a024d742e5b45fb3c5d10fd3b2),
[Dergipark](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/3702828)). **We must not turn these into claims on the site.**

## 3. Value proposition

> **Five patient-education videos every Monday, on what patients in your country actually search for, in your clinic's
> colours, checked for health-advertising rules and clinical accuracy before you see them. You approve in one tap and share in one tap.**

Why it is believable: these are real product facts (see `docs/AUTONOMOUS_PLAN.md`):
- automatic Monday delivery;
- topics taken from Google suggestions in the clinic's country;
- two reviewers, one for health-advertising rules and one for clinical accuracy;
- a sound and picture check;
- the clinic's brand colours and logo;
- an approve-all button;
- an email in seven languages;
- share from the phone in one tap;
- the dentist's own 5-minute recording turned into captioned clips.

## 7. Claims we must NOT make (our own marketing is health-adjacent advertising too)

- **No guaranteed outcomes:** not "X new patients a month", "fill your chair", "double your bookings", "ROI guaranteed".
  ROI is framed only as "one case covers the plan", with "your results will differ".
- **No legal guarantees:** not "100% compliant", "HWG-approved", "approved by the ASA/GDC/TDB/Ordre", "legally safe", or
  "replaces legal advice". Say instead: "checked against the rules; you approve; we're not a law firm".
- **No endorsements we don't have.** Do not name professional bodies as partners.
- **No invented statistics or testimonials** (the ground rules in `docs/AUTONOMOUS_PLAN.md`). Use real clinic quotes only with written consent.
- **No disparaging named competitors.** The comparison is with categories ("an agency", "doing it yourself") and stays fair: "depends on
  the agency — ask who checks it", not "agencies break the law".
- **Be honest about AI:** don't imply a human dentist wrote or voiced the videos. Don't say "no AI". Don't claim that stock footage never
  shows people unless the filter keeps it true. Today it is limited to object shots, and the dentist's approval is still required.
- **No "auto-posting"** until the Meta/TikTok integrations are approved and live.
- **No "permanent price" or price lock.** There is no founding price any more; list prices may change with 30 days' notice (Terms).
