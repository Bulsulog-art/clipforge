# TikTok settings checklist

Menu names are from memory unless a source is given and may differ by app version and region. Where TikTok's help
site would be the source, the entry is marked **unverified**: support.tiktok.com could not be fetched from this
session. Tick each box in the app and note anything that differs.

Settings live under **Profile → menu (three lines) → Settings and privacy** in the app, and under the profile menu →
**Settings** on tiktok.com.

## 1. Security (do this before the first post)

- [ ] **Password:** long and unique, generated by the company password manager. Never in chat or a doc.
- [ ] **2-step verification:** Settings and privacy → Security (or "Security and permissions") → 2-step verification.
      Turn on **authenticator app** (stored in the company password manager) **and email** (hello@dentvideo.com).
      Add SMS only if the company phone number is permanent. TikTok may ask you to choose at least two methods
      (**unverified**). Save any backup codes in the password manager.
- [ ] **Security alerts** on; review **Manage devices** and remove any device you do not recognise.
- [ ] Do not stay logged in on shared or borrowed computers. Log out of the browser session at the end of setup.
- [ ] **Account recovery** reaches hello@dentvideo.com, which must itself have 2-step verification.

## 3. Comment filters

- [ ] **Filter all comments:** off (we want to read dentists' comments).
- [ ] **Filter spam and offensive comments:** on.
- [ ] **Filter keywords:** on, with this starting list:
  `bahis, casino, kumar, kripto, crypto, takipçi, followers, follow back, beğeni satın, promo, giveaway, çekiliş, kazandınız, dm me, whatsapp grubu, link in my bio`
  Add words that show up. Do not filter "fiyat", "ücret" or "price": people asking the price are real leads; answer
  them in words with the plan names and prices as on the site, never with a discount.
- [ ] **Comment replies, house rules** (keep a note in the phone):
  - A patient asking about their own teeth: "Bunu en iyi kendi diş hekiminiz değerlendirir. Biz kliniklere video
    hazırlayan bir yazılımız, tanı ya da tedavi önerisi veremeyiz." No diagnosis, no advice, no clinic referral.
  - Never reply with a patient story, a result or a satisfaction claim.
  - Hide (not delete) a comment that names a real clinic, a dentist or a patient, or shares health data.
- [ ] **Q&A on profile:** off at launch.

## 4. Content disclosure and branded content

- [ ] **"Disclose post content" → "Your brand"**: on for every DentVideo post. TikTok's Community Guidelines
      (Regulated Goods, Services, and Commercial Activities, effective 24 Sep 2026, read from tiktok.com):
      "If you're posting commercial content on TikTok, you must clearly disclose it using the content disclosure
      setting." This includes "Promoting your own business, product, or service". Undisclosed commercial content
      "may reduce its visibility", and repeated failure can restrict or ban the account.
- [ ] **"Branded content" (the third-party toggle): off.** It is for posts made for another brand in exchange for
      payment or an incentive. We do not do that.
- [ ] **No paid creator or dentist partnerships** without the owner's approval and a manual read of the Branded
      Content Policy. Its **prohibited industries** include "Any medical institutions, treatments or devices" and
      "Teeth whitening products" (read from tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy). DentVideo is software, but any
      post where a partner dentist's clinic or a whitening product is promoted falls straight into that list, and TR
      law adds its own limits (KIT section 5.2).
- [ ] **Promote / ads:** do not use. No spending; and TikTok's healthcare ad policy needs a manual read first.

## 5. TikTok health and medical rules that apply to us

From the Community Guidelines (released 25 Aug 2026, effective 24 Sep 2026), read from tiktok.com with curl:

1. **Health misinformation.** Not allowed: "Health misinformation that could cause significant harm, such as
   promoting unproven treatments ... discouraging professional care". Not eligible for the For You feed: "Health
   misinformation that can result in moderate harm, such as false claims about treating non-life threatening
   conditions". Our samples must match mainstream dental guidance and always say "genel bilgi verir, muayenenin yerini
   tutmaz". Never imply a viewer's own condition ("Diş etiniz kanıyor mu?" as a hook is out).
2. **Regulated services.** "Restricted Medical Services" (diagnosis or treatment needing a licence) and "Invasive
   Cosmetic Procedures" (cutting the skin or using needles to change appearance) may not be traded, marketed or
   accessed through TikTok, except that "licensed and regulated medical entities with a verified business account"
   may be allowed in some regions. **We are not a medical entity and do not sell treatment.** So: never offer, price or
   book a treatment; never link to a clinic; keep every post about the software. At launch, avoid the implant and
   whitening samples; use everyday hygiene topics.
3. **Commercial disclosure** (section 4 above).
4. **AI-generated content** (POSTS.md, last section): label realistic AI; generic TTS narration is exempt by the rule,
   but our policy labels it when audible.
5. **Unoriginal content.** "Content is also ineligible for the FYF if it includes unoriginal or reused material
   without anything new." Never post a sample as the whole video; never re-upload an export carrying another app's
   watermark.
6. **Minors.** No children on screen. Do not use the DE/PL child-patient samples (kinder-zahnarztangst,
   pierwsza-wizyta-dziecka) on TikTok.
7. **Deceptive behaviour.** No bought followers or engagement, no automation tools, no second account to work around a
   restriction (same guidelines, "Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement").

Turkish law (Sağlık Hizmetlerinde Tanıtım ve Bilgilendirme Faaliyetleri Hakkında Yönetmelik, RG 12.11.2025, no. 33075)
applies on top: no real identified dentist, no patient thanks or satisfaction, no price, campaign or superlative in
anything a clinic would publish. KIT section 5.2 has the full list; it is our reading, not legal advice.
