# DentVideo's own short video: what reaches dentists, and 12 concepts made for them

Written 2026-11-17. Audience: **dentists, clinic owners and their teams** (not patients). Goal: DentVideo's own
TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts content that dental professionals watch to the end, send to a colleague,
and that shows the product correctly.

**Owner direction (2026-11-16), applied throughout:**

- Nothing may look or feel AI-made. Human, clever, crafted, original: wit, a surprising angle, a strong visual
  metaphor, real clinic situations, honest behind-the-scenes. No template "AI tool demo", no stock-voice slideshow.
- **No numbers, no price comparisons, no stats in the content.** Show what the product does for a clinic and how it
  feels to use it. If the product is shown well, it sells.
- The repo rules still apply (`CLAUDE.md`): no invented customers, testimonials, results, "best" or "#1"; every clinic in
  our footage is a fictional sample clinic and is labelled as one; the check is "a careful filter, not legal advice";
  the dentist approves every video.

**How this was researched.** Free sources only: web search, YouTube's public search pages (read without signing in,
2026-11-16/17), YouTube's public autocomplete endpoint, TikTok's public Creative Center and Creative Codes PDF, and the
public policy pages of YouTube, TikTok and Meta. Not used: Firecrawl or any paid tool, any login. TikTok and Instagram
feeds themselves cannot be read without an account, so per-video numbers below come from **YouTube**, where the same
clips are often cross-posted. View counts are rounded as YouTube displayed them; since 31.03.2025 a Shorts "view"
counts every start or replay with no minimum watch time (YouTube Help, *Get started creating YouTube Shorts*), so
compare them with each other, not with older long-form numbers. Where a source is a vendor blog, it is marked as such.

## 0. Ten findings in one screen

1. **Dentists share in-jokes about their own working day, not marketing advice.** Clinic-team humour (dentist vs
   assistant, front desk, "everyone in dentistry knows this moment") reaches tens to hundreds of thousands of views in
   EN, TR and DE. Dental *business-advice* Shorts sit at tens to a few thousand. Dental *software demos* sit at single
   or double digits. Evidence in §1.
2. **The professional-audience model is Dr. Glaucomflecken:** insider-specific sketches satirising the *system*
   (insurers, billing, admin), never the patient. His recent Shorts read 114K to 1.5M views. Physicians describe it as
   recognition, not entertainment. That is the tone to borrow, with the clinic's admin load as the "system".
3. **Clinic "tanıtım" films and corporate clinic reels barely move** (hundreds of views in TR). Polish without an idea
   does not travel. Our concepts need an idea first and polish second.
4. **Patient-facing megacreators (The Bentist, The Braces Guy, Dental Digest, Dr. Winters) are not our channel.** They
   reach patients by the million. Dentists watch them too, but nothing public separates that share.
5. **Great B2B/product films sell through a situation, not a feature list:** Slack's "So Yeah, We Tried Slack…"
   (a team talking about its own day), Apple at Work's *The Underdogs* (the product lives inside a sitcom), IKEA's
   "bookbook" (a tech-keynote parody about a paper catalogue). All three are human, specific and self-aware.
6. **TikTok's own creative research:** land the proposition early (the hook carries most of the recall), brand subtly in
   the hook, build hook → body → close, feature people, shoot native 9:16, use text overlays, and treat sound as half
   the experience (TikTok *Creative Codes*).
7. **Search is real but narrow.** Clinic owners in TR and DE do not type "marketing" into YouTube; they type
   "diş kliniği tanıtım videosu", "diş kliniği otomasyonu", "zahnarztpraxis gründen", "praxisübernahme". English
   owners do type "dental marketing ideas for dentists", "dental instagram story ideas", "dentist reels". §3.5.
8. **Music:** on business accounts, use only the platform's commercial library (TikTok Commercial Music Library, Meta
   Sound Collection, YouTube Audio Library) or our own sound. Licences do not travel between platforms, so export our
   masters **without music** and add it natively per platform. Better still for the "human, crafted" direction: real
   clinic sound design (drill whine, glove snap, chair hiss, keyboard) as the soundtrack.
9. **Platform rules that bite us:** realistic AI audio/video must be labelled on YouTube and TikTok, and Meta labels it
   too; reused or unoriginal material is ineligible for TikTok's For You feed and loses recommendations on Instagram.
   Our weekly sample videos are synthetic-voice and template-built, so they are **ingredients**, never the post itself.
10. **The concept that best fits all of the above:** "Same sentence, all day" (§6, #1): one dentist repeating the same
    aftercare explanation from the first patient to the last, then sending it as the clinic's own video instead. Pure
    recognition, a real product moment, no numbers, no AI look.

### 1e. Gaps nobody fills (our opening)

- Nobody makes **crafted** content about the clinic's *communication and marketing burden*: the Instagram nobody has
  time for, the explanation repeated all day, the fear of breaking ad rules. Team humour covers chairside life; the
  admin life is untouched apart from a few low-reach DE clips.
- Dental software brands post either demos or stock "burnout" ads. None uses the insider-sketch voice.
- No one shows a dental product with tabletop craft (stop-motion, real props, practical light). Everything is screen or
  talking head.

## 3. Hooks, length, pacing, text, captions, timing, search

### 3.1 What TikTok's own research says (primary source)

TikTok *Creative Codes* ([PDF, ads.tiktok.com](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf)),
based on TikTok Marketing Science / Kantar coding of TikTok ads:

- "The first six seconds are vital": the hook carries most of the recall and awareness effect. "Land the content value
  proposition early."
- Hook types it names: **suspense**, **surprise**, **emotion**.
- "Branding in the first few seconds should be subtle and not compromise the hook"; structure **hook → body →
  close**, with a clear call to action at the close.
- Shoot 9:16, feature people (creators, employees), use TikTok-native text overlays, adopt the category's own norms.
- Sound: "sound-on is powerful". Music sets mood and rhythm, voiceover reveals story detail, and **sounds parallel or
  amplify the action** ("ASMR, demonstrations, candid camera"; "ensure sounds are high-quality").

### 3.2 Length and pacing

- **Target 15–25 s, hard cap 30 s.** Sketches with one turn (§1a) mostly run 8–20 s. The existing social kit already
  caps at 30 s.
- **First frame is already the situation**, mid-action: no logo intro, no "Hi, I'm…", no title card.
- **One idea per video.** A cut every 1–2 s in montage beats; let a sketch beat breathe (a held reaction is the joke).
- **The product appears after the turn**, not before. Logo only at the close (1–2 s) or as a prop in the scene.
- **Loop**: make the last frame lead back into the first (e.g. "same sentence, all day" ends on the next patient).

### 3.3 On-screen text and captions

- One line of hook text in the first second, in the native text style of each app (it reads as native, per TikTok).
- Burn-in captions for every spoken line: professionals watch between patients, often with sound off. Keep them in the
  safe area (TikTok/Reels UI covers the bottom ~20% and the right edge).
- Write captions in the voice of the scene, not of a brand: "Me, explaining whitening sensitivity for the 9th time
  today" is a number; the no-numbers version is "Me, explaining whitening sensitivity. Again."
- Post caption: one plain sentence + who it's for + the sample line where samples appear + 3–5 hashtags.

  [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/best-times-to-post-2025-sprout-social/744014/)).
  Healthcare organisations: Instagram Tue and Fri 08–10 ([Hootsuite, healthcare benchmarks](https://blog.hootsuite.com/healthcare-social-media-benchmarks)).
- Our viewers are *at the chair* in clinic hours. Hypothesis: **lunch break (12:30–14:00 local) and evening
  (20:00–22:00 local)**, plus Sunday evening for "Monday" themed posts.

  - US: post for Eastern 12:00–13:00 and 20:00, which lands in the late morning/evening further west.

## 4. Music: what a business account may use

- **TikTok.** Business accounts get the **Commercial Music Library (CML)**, which TikTok describes as "pre-cleared music
  for organic content and ad creation" ([Creative Center CML](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/music/pc/en)).
  The general library of chart songs is for personal, non-commercial use; business accounts cannot use it, and cannot
  duet or stitch videos that use it (secondary sources: [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/tiktok-music-licensing-rules),
  [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-changes-rules-on-music-usage-by-businesses/577734/)).
  CML clearance covers TikTok only.
- **Instagram/Facebook.** Business accounts see a limited, royalty-free catalogue (Meta Sound Collection) instead of the
  mainstream licensed catalogue; Sound Collection tracks are cleared for Facebook and Instagram only
  ([Foxi Music guide](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/),
  [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/instagram-music-library)). Meta's own help pages were not reachable
  from this session; check in the app before relying on details.
- **YouTube Shorts.** YouTube Audio Library tracks, or Shorts audio offered in the app; anything else risks Content ID
  claims.
- **Rule for us:**
  1. Export every master **without music**. Add the platform's own commercial track inside each app.
  2. Never a trending chart song, never a "sped-up" copy of one.
  3. Prefer **our own sound**: recorded clinic foley (glove snap, suction, chair hydraulics, a drill in the next room,
     keyboard taps, a WhatsApp send), the owner humming, a single piano note. This is free, legal everywhere, and is
     exactly what makes a video feel made by a person.
  4. The synthetic narration from sample videos is not music, but it is AI audio: see §5.

## 5. Platform rules relevant to us

### 5a. AI-generated content labelling

- **YouTube** (official, [Help: disclosing altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)):
  "We require creators to disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic." Not required for
  "non-realistic content", minor aesthetic edits, "production assistance, like using generative AI tools to create or
  improve a video outline, script, thumbnail, title, or infographic", or "cloning one's own voice to create voice overs
  or dubs". Labels may appear "in the expanded description for non-music AI-generated audio".
- **TikTok** (official, Community Guidelines, *Integrity and Authenticity*): creators must "label AI-generated or
  significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people. Unlabeled content may be removed,
  restricted, or labeled by our team."
- **Meta** (official, [Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/)):
  "AI info" labels when Meta detects industry-standard AI indicators or when people self-disclose; content only edited
  by AI moves the label into the post menu.
- **Our policy:** any post containing a sample clinic video (synthetic narration) gets the AI label on every platform
  and says so in the caption, as the existing social kit already does (08-social-launch-kit.md, rule 4). The new
  concepts mostly avoid the issue by using **real people, real sound and real screens**; the synthetic voice appears
  only when we show the product's output, and then it is labelled. That is also the honest answer to "never look
  AI-made": we don't hide the AI; we keep it in its place (the product's output), and everything around it is human.

### 5b. Unoriginal / reused content

- **TikTok** (official, Community Guidelines): "Content is also ineligible for the FYF if it includes unoriginal or
  reused material without anything new." The For You feed standards (effective 24.09.2026) add that videos left out of
  the feed can still be found through search or the profile, and that TikTok analytics shows when a video was made
  ineligible.
- **Instagram:** since 2022, ranking credits original creators and cuts reach for aggregators; in spring 2026 Mosseri
  extended this beyond Reels to photos and carousels: accounts that mostly post unoriginal material stop being
  recommended to non-followers; near-identical reposts are swapped for the original
  ([PetaPixel, 30.04.2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/),
  [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-its-original-content-policy),
  [TechCrunch/Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/04/21/instagram-adam-mosseri-algorithm-change-original-content/)).
  Practice: upload clean files, never a TikTok export with its watermark.
- **YouTube:** from 15.07.2025 "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content": mass-produced or repetitive
  material, e.g. one template with minimal differences, is not monetisable. This affects monetisation, not hosting
  ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/),
  [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)).
  We do not monetise, but the same signal hurts recommendation.
- **Consequence:** a feed of our weekly sample videos posted as-is would look templated and mass-produced. Samples are
  footage *inside* original pieces (a phone screen in a scene, a triptych, a 3-second payoff), never the whole post.

### 5c. Health-related content and ads

- **Organic posts** that show sample patient-education videos are health information in public. Keep the lines the
  existing kit uses: fictional sample clinic, "general information, not a substitute for an examination", no promised
  results, no before/after of real patients, no patient faces without written consent (sketch actors are not patients
  and are introduced as the team).
- **Clinic-advertising law** stays relevant even though *we* sell software: our sample content must itself pass the
  rules we claim to check. TR: Sağlık Hizmetlerinde Tanıtım ve Bilgilendirme Yönetmeliği; DE: Heilmittelwerbegesetz
  (HWG) and professional codes; both are covered in `docs/gtm/02-outreach-law-and-channels.md`. In content, the check
  is always "a careful filter, not legal advice".
- **Paid promotion (if ever approved):** Meta's health and wellness ad policy restricts before/after imagery and copy
  that asserts or implies a viewer's personal health attributes; TikTok has a separate healthcare ad policy. Both
  pages blocked automated reading from this session (`transparency.meta.com`, TikTok ads help returned no content),
  so verify them by hand before any paid boost. Paid spend needs the owner's approval in any case.
- **Never** say "painless", "guaranteed", "the best", "#1", or a result. The red-pen concept (§6 #5) turns this
  into content.

## 6. Twelve original DentVideo concepts, ranked

**Ranking logic:** (1) how strongly a dentist or assistant will recognise themselves and send it to a colleague
(§1a evidence), (2) how correctly and beautifully the product is shown, (3) effort. All 15–30 s, 9:16. Every concept
obeys: no numbers, no prices, no stats, no claims; fictional sample clinic named as such on screen; the real product
screens only (the app's real approve flow, the real logo preview at dentvideo.com, real sample videos); "the dentist
approves every video" where the check appears.

**What we can make ourselves:** real screen recordings (the dev harness used for the social kit, see
08-social-launch-kit.md), our real sample videos, motion typography (Remotion, as in `scripts/social-kit/`), tabletop
and object shots on a phone, recorded foley. **What needs people:** marked per concept. Hands-only roles can be played
by the owner. A white coat, gloves and a dental chair can be borrowed from any clinic for an hour; no real patient is
ever filmed.

**One metric for all:** **shares + sends per 1,000 reach** (TikTok "shares", Instagram "sends", YouTube "shares").
That is the signal that a professional passed it on. Secondary: saves, profile visits, and clicks to the logo
preview through a per-platform UTM link. Views alone mislead (patients and random viewers inflate them).

---

### 1. Same sentence, all day

- **Idea:** A dentist says the identical aftercare sentence to patient after patient, from morning coffee to evening
  lights-off; at the last patient she sends it instead, as her clinic's own video.
- **Why fresh:** Every dentist has this sentence. Nobody has turned the repetition itself into the joke, and the
  product is the punchline, not an ad break.
- **Beats (24 s):** 0–1 s: close-up, mouth under mask: "Avoid coffee and red wine for a while after whitening…" (hook text: "Every
  dentist has one sentence"). 1–14 s: jump cuts of the same line, same framing, as the light through the window shifts
  morning → noon → dusk; the coffee cup on the counter goes full → empty → full; her delivery gets flatter; one cut she
  mouths it along with the patient. 14–18 s: last patient, she opens her mouth to say it… stops, picks up the phone.
  18–23 s: over-the-shoulder: the phone shows the clinic's video on that exact topic (real sample, fictional clinic
  badge visible), she taps send. 23–24 s: she drinks the coffee. End card: "DentVideo · every Monday, five videos in
  your clinic's voice."
- **Ourselves:** the phone screen (real sample video), end card, sound.
- **Person on camera:** one dentist (or actor in scrubs), one "patient" seen from behind. Half a day in a borrowed
  treatment room; window light does the time-lapse work.
- **Sound:** no music until the send; one CML piano note on the send. Ambient clinic sound throughout.
- **Effort:** medium. **Metric:** shares per reach; comments naming *their* sentence (a reply video series
  writes itself).

### 2. The dormant feed

- **Idea:** A clinic's Instagram, scrolled slowly like a crime-scene pan: the last post is "Happy New Year" with a
  party hat on a tooth model. Dust settles on the phone. Then Monday.
- **Why fresh:** Every clinic owner knows their neglected account and feels guilty. Shown with deadpan cinematography
  rather than a lecture, it is a confession, not an accusation.
- **Beats (20 s):** 0–2 s: macro of a phone on a reception desk, a real dust layer (flour) on the glass, a finger wipes
  it; hook text "Our clinic's last post." 2–8 s: screen recording of a **fictional** clinic profile (Örnek Diş Kliniği
  / Beispiel-Zahnarztpraxis), slow scroll through a few faded old posts, ending on the party-hat tooth. 8–10 s: a
  notification slides in: Monday. 10–17 s: the same fictional grid fills, tile by tile, with sample covers, a soft
  whoosh each. 17–20 s: the receptionist (hands only) wipes the rest of the dust off. End card.
- **Ourselves:** everything: the mock profile is built from our own covers (08 kit's grid contact sheets), dust on a
  real phone, foley. **Person:** hands only.
- **Rules:** the grid shows covers, not numbers (no likes, no follower count visible). "Sample clinic" badge.
- **Effort:** low. **Metric:** sends per reach; saves.

### 3. "Can you also do the Instagram?"

- **Idea:** A two-person team sketch in the Glaucomflecken style: the assistant, mid-procedure, asks the dentist to
  "also do the Instagram"; the dentist's face above the mask says everything. Part 1 of a running series.
- **Why fresh:** Team-contrast sketches are the top format among dental staff (§1a), but nobody has aimed one at the
  clinic's content burden, and nobody has made it a recurring character pair.
- **Beats (18 s):** 0–2 s: two-shot over the chair (patient out of frame, a dental mannequin head works), assistant:
  "Hocam, Instagram'a da bir şey atsak?" 2–6 s: the dentist freezes, slow push-in on the eyes above the mask; the
  suction keeps hissing. 6–10 s: smash cut to the dentist at 23:00 at the kitchen table, ring light, trying to film a
  brushing video; the dog walks through the shot. 10–15 s: back in the clinic, Monday morning: the assistant shows her
  phone: five videos waiting, "just tap approve". The dentist taps it with an elbow (gloves on). 15–18 s: the
  assistant, deadpan: "Şimdi TikTok'a da…" Cut.
- **Ourselves:** the approve screen insert (real app). **People:** two on camera (dentist + assistant). Any clinic
  team the owner knows can play it; credit them by first name with permission.
- **Series:** Part 2 "…and in German?", Part 3 "…is this allowed in an ad?" (leads into #5).
- **Effort:** medium. **Metric:** shares per reach; follow rate on Part 2.

### 4. Gloves still on

- **Idea:** POV, chairside: the week's videos are approved between two patients, with the dentist never taking her
  gloves off: knuckle taps, a pen as a stylus, an elbow for the last one.
- **Why fresh:** It shows the real approve flow (the actual product), but the craft is in the physical comedy and the
  POV framing. It answers "when would I even have time?" without saying a number.
- **Beats (16 s):** 0–1 s: POV of gloved hands, the phone propped on the instrument tray; text "Approving this week's
  videos." 1–11 s: real app screens (iPhone, filmed in-scene, not just captured): video plays a second, knuckle tap
  approve; next; pen tap; next; elbow; the fifth one the assistant's hand reaches in and taps. Each approve gets a
  tray-instrument "clink". 11–14 s: the next patient's footsteps; the phone goes face down. 14–16 s: end card "Nothing
  is posted until you approve."
- **Ourselves:** almost all (owner's gloved hands, a phone on a tray, the iOS app in a test account with the
  fictional clinic). **Person:** hands only.
- **Effort:** low. **Metric:** sends per reach; comments like "my assistant would do the last one".

### 5. The red pen

- **Idea:** An old-school copy editor's red pen marks up a clinic-ad script on paper: "painless" struck through,
  "guaranteed" circled, "the best clinic in town" crossed out with a sigh; then the same script appears in the app's
  real check screen with the same flags.
- **Why fresh:** Ad-rule anxiety is real for clinics (HWG, the Turkish regulation), but every explainer is a lawyer
  talking. Physical paper, a fountain-pen scratch sound and a deadpan editor's hand make a dry rule satisfying to watch.
- **Beats (22 s):** 0–2 s: macro, nib touches paper, text "Words that don't belong in a dental ad." 2–12 s: four
  strikes, each with a margin note in handwriting ("promise", "superlative", "fear", "result"). Scratch sounds, no
  music. 12–18 s: match cut from paper to the real check view in the app, same flagged words, rewritten line below.
  18–22 s: hand caps the pen. Text: "A careful filter, not legal advice. You approve every video."
- **Ourselves:** all of it (paper, pen, phone camera on a tripod, real screen recording). **Person:** hands only.
- **Per market:** TR words and DE words differ; shoot one paper per language.
- **Effort:** low. **Metric:** saves (people keep rule content) and shares.

### 6. Five teeth on a Monday (tabletop stop-motion)

- **Idea:** Five white tooth models shuffle across a reception counter in stop-motion like a little delivery, each one
  "unrolls" a tiny phone-shaped card that plays a real sample video; the last one bows.
- **Why fresh:** No one in dental marketing uses practical, handmade animation. Stop-motion is visibly made by hands,
  which is the opposite of an AI look, and it is a visual metaphor for "five videos arrive".
- **Beats (20 s):** 0–2 s: an empty counter, a bell "ding" (front-desk bell), text "Monday." 2–10 s: five tooth models
  hop in from off-frame, one per beat, in rhythm with the bell. 10–16 s: one by one they tilt back to reveal a phone
  on the counter playing 1-second clips of five different sample videos (fictional clinic, AI label in the post).
  16–19 s: the fifth tooth bows; a gloved finger nudges it upright. 19–20 s: end card.
- **Ourselves:** all of it: tooth models are cheap, a phone on a tripod with a stop-motion app, 6–8 fps. Sample clips
  are real. **Person:** none (one hand at the end).
- **Effort:** medium (a day of patient animation). **Metric:** shares per reach; average watch time (stop-motion loops
  well).

### 7. Your logo, before anything

- **Idea:** An owner (seen from behind) drags their logo file into dentvideo.com on a laptop at the reception after
  closing; the real preview renders with the clinic's colours; the overhead light switches off; the screen lights her
  face.
- **Why fresh:** It shows the free logo preview, which is a real, first-touch product moment, as a small private
  moment rather than a feature tour. The craft is in the light.
- **Beats (18 s):** 0–2 s: dark reception, laptop glow, text "Your logo. On a video. Before you sign up for anything."
  2–10 s: over-the-shoulder, real screen: logo dropped, preview builds (fictional clinic logo, e.g. Beispiel-
  Zahnarztpraxis monogram). 10–15 s: the vertical preview plays; the light timer clicks off; only the screen remains.
  15–18 s: she leans back. End card: "Free logo preview at dentvideo.com."
- **Ourselves:** all (owner in the chair from behind, a real laptop, the real preview flow). **Person:** back of a
  head.
- **Rules:** the preview is free; the copy must not call it a free video or free trial.
- **Effort:** low. **Metric:** clicks to the logo preview (UTM), then shares.

### 8. Lost in translation (the jargon cut)

- **Idea:** The dentist says a clinical sentence; the subtitles show what the patient actually heard ("periodontal
  pocket" → "a pocket… in my gum? for what?"); then the clinic's plain-language video answers the same question.
- **Why fresh:** Every dentist has watched a patient nod without understanding. The joke is on jargon, never on the
  patient, which keeps it on the right side (Glaucomflecken's rule: satirise the system, not patients).
- **Beats (22 s):** 0–2 s: dentist to camera (chairside angle): "We'll need to treat the periodontal pockets." Text
  "What patients hear:". 2–9 s: subtitles below, the misheard version, illustrated with a quick, hand-drawn doodle
  overlay (a literal pocket on a tooth). The patient (from behind) nods slowly. 9–12 s: the dentist sees the nod and
  sighs. 12–20 s: the phone in the patient's hand plays the sample video on that topic (real, fictional clinic, AI
  label). 20–22 s: patient: "Ohh." End card.
- **Ourselves:** doodles (hand-drawn on an iPad, scanned), the sample video. **Person:** dentist on camera (the
  owner could voice it off-camera with hands in shot), a patient from behind.
- **Effort:** medium. **Metric:** shares; comments adding their own jargon (feeds a series per language).

### 9. The Monday ritual

- **Idea:** A ritual film, ASMR-precise, of a clinic opening on Monday: blinds up, autoclave beep, coffee, chair
  hiss, and on the reception phone, the week's videos quietly arrive. No voice. Pure sound design.
- **Why fresh:** Clinic-opening routines exist as vlogs, but nobody has shot one as a crafted sound piece where the
  product is one more sound in the morning symphony.
- **Beats (20 s):** 0–1 s: key in the door (click). 1–12 s: eight close-ups, one sound each: blinds, light switch,
  autoclave beep, compressor start, coffee machine, chair hydraulics, glove box, keyboard. 12–16 s: the phone on the
  counter: notification sound, the real "this week's videos are ready" screen, thumb opens the first one, two seconds
  play. 16–20 s: first patient's shadow at the door. End card.
- **Ourselves:** all, with a clinic for an hour before opening and a good phone mic. **Person:** hands only.
- **Sound:** recorded foley only; CML ambient bed on TikTok optional.
- **Effort:** low-medium. **Metric:** completion rate and saves; shares are secondary for this one.

### 10. "Introducing: Approve" (keynote parody)

- **Idea:** A dead-serious product-keynote reveal (slow orbiting light, a black stage, reverent voiceover by the owner)
  of the most advanced feature in modern dentistry: a thumb tapping "approve".
- **Why fresh:** IKEA's "bookbook" proved a keynote parody of an ordinary thing travels far. In dental B2B nobody has
  dared it. It also makes the key promise memorable: nothing goes out until the dentist approves.
- **Beats (25 s):** 0–2 s: black, a single spotlight on a phone face down; owner's whisper: "Introducing…". 2–12 s:
  slow-motion macro of a thumb; spec-sheet style typography in the empty space: "Single tap." "Works with gloves."
  "Says no, too." 12–18 s: the real approve screen, the tap, a cinematic boom (CML sound effect). 18–23 s: a "reject
  with a note" tap, same reverence. 23–25 s: "Nothing goes out without it." End card.
- **Ourselves:** all: a black cloth, one lamp, macro on a phone, motion typography, the owner's own voice. **Person:**
  a thumb and a voice.
- **Rules:** no spec numbers in the parody (no "2× faster"). One post, not a series.
- **Effort:** medium (lighting). **Metric:** shares per reach.

### 11. Behind the five (honest making-of)

- **Idea:** How one patient-education video is really made, shown as a physical production board: index cards for
  script, check, voice, captions, render, and the dentist's approval, each card flipped to reveal the real screen
  behind it, including the honest card: "The voice is synthetic. That's why you approve."
- **Why fresh:** BTS from a software company is usually a slick animation. Index cards, a cork board and the founder's
  handwriting make it human, and saying the uncomfortable part out loud builds trust with professionals.
- **Beats (28 s):** 0–2 s: hand pins the first card, text "How one of our videos is actually made." 2–22 s: six cards,
  ~3 s each: card flips → 2 s of the real screen (script text, check flags, voice waveform, caption timing, the vertical
  render, the approve tap). The voice card is held a beat longer. 22–28 s: the finished sample plays small, pinned in
  the middle of the board. End card.
- **Ourselves:** all (cork board, cards, real screens). **Person:** hands; optionally the owner's voice.
- **Rules:** AI label on; say "synthetic voice", "careful filter, not legal advice", "you approve every video".
- **Effort:** low-medium. **Metric:** saves, profile visits, comments with questions (a follow-up Q&A source).

### 12. One question, three languages

- **Idea:** A vertical triptych: the same patient question ("Is whitening sensitivity normal?") answered at the same
  moment by three sample clinics in Turkish, German and Spanish, each in its own colours, cut so the three voices
  overlap like a round, then align on one shared word.
- **Why fresh:** It shows a real differentiator (the clinic's own language, the clinic's own brand) as a visual rhyme,
  not a claim. Musical editing gives it craft.
- **Beats (18 s):** 0–2 s: three narrow panels, three logos (all fictional sample clinics, labelled), text "Same
  question. Three clinics. Three languages." 2–14 s: the three real sample videos play in sync, cut on matching
  gestures and scene changes. 14–16 s: the panels slide together on a shared word. 16–18 s: end card.
- **Ourselves:** all, from real sample renders (only topics that exist in all three languages in the preview library;
  see the on-hold note in 08 for TR aligner). **Person:** none.
- **Rules:** AI label on (synthetic voices), "sample clinics" on screen, "general information, not a substitute for an
  examination" in the caption.
- **Effort:** low. **Metric:** shares from non-TR markets; profile visits from DE/ES.

## 7. Production notes that make it feel human

- **Shoot on a phone, light like a film.** Window light, one practical lamp, a clean background. Avoid ring-light
  "influencer" lighting except where it is the joke (#3).
- **Real sound first.** Record foley in a real clinic; music only from the platform's commercial library, added in-app.
- **Real hands, real props.** Paper, pens, tooth models, a cork board, dust. Physical things read as human.
- **Product shots are short and true.** 2–6 s of a real screen, filmed in-scene on a real device where possible, or a
  clean capture from the dev harness. Never a mock-up of a feature that doesn't exist.
- **No AI imagery or AI video in our own marketing.** Generative visuals would contradict the brief and need labels.
  The synthetic narration appears only inside sample videos, labelled.
- **Captions by hand,** in the native text style of each app; burn-ins checked for typos in all three languages.
- **Fictional clinics are named as fictional, on screen**, every time. No made-up handle (it could be a real account).
- **People in sketches are a real team who agreed**; no patients; no faces of anyone who has not signed a release.
